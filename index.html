Arborist Assistant

Put your trees on your plot plan. Record what you find — measurements, condition, photos, and voice notes — and build a picture that grows every time you walk the property.

What it looks like

Your own survey sits underneath. Each tree is a numbered marker you can tap. Once you tell the app the scale, it draws each tree’s canopy and its root zone at true size, so you can see what is really overhanging the house.

Getting started

About ten minutes at the kitchen table, then you are ready to walk.

  1. Open it and import your plot plan

    Press Import plan and choose a picture of your survey — a photo of a paper copy works. If yours is a PDF, save it as an image first.

  2. Tell it the scale

    Press Set scale, drag a line along something you know the length of — the scale bar on the survey, or a property line — and type in that length. This is what lets the app draw canopies and root zones at true size.

  3. Drop a marker on each tree

    Press + Tree, then tap where the tree is. Fill in the species and the trunk size. You can type the measurement across the trunk, or the distance around it — whichever way you measured.

  4. Take photos and record what is said

    Photograph each tree, and draw straight on the picture to mark where a cut should go. Press Record to capture the conversation and type it up later.

  5. Press Save package before you finish

    You get one .zip file holding everything — the plan, the data, the photos and the recordings. That file is your record. Keep it somewhere safe.

Coming back next year? Open your saved file, start a new visit, and walk round again. Last year’s numbers are already filled in, so you only change what has changed — and the app shows you the growth.

What it keeps track of

For each tree

  • Species, trunk size, height and spread
  • Condition and how urgent any work is
  • Defects seen and work recommended
  • Multiple trunks, totalled the way the city expects
  • Photos, with lines drawn on to show the cuts
  • Voice notes, ready to be typed up

For the property

  • Your survey as the background
  • Canopy and root zones drawn to scale
  • How much of a root zone the house or drive covers
  • Whether a tree is likely protected under Austin’s rules
  • A printable schedule for the client
  • A spreadsheet export

Using it on your phone

Open this page on your phone and add it to your home screen. After that it opens like any other app and works with no signal at all — useful on a property with poor reception.

  • iPhone: Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
  • Android: open the app, go to the Project tab and press Install app.

Adding it to the home screen also tells the phone to hold on to your photos and recordings properly, so it is worth doing before a real survey.

Where your information goes

Nowhere. This page hands your phone the app and nothing else. There is no account, no server, and no company holding your data.

Everything you record stays on the device you recorded it on, and in the .zip files you save yourself. Because of that, the phone and the computer keep separate copies — moving work between them means saving a package and opening it on the other one.

A few honest limits

  • The app tells you when a tree looks protected under Austin’s rules, but that is a prompt to check, not a ruling. Confirm anything that matters with the City Arborist.
  • Markers are placed by eye on your plan. That is fine for planning and for talking to an arborist; it is not a surveyed position.
  • Saving your package is the real backup. Browser storage is durable, but it is not forever.
